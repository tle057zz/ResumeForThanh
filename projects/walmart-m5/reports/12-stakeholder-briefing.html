1. Bottom line for the boss

Demand is healthy when you read it on a daily basis. Average daily units rose from about 37.8K in 2015 to about 41.8K in 2016 year-to-date (through 22 May). Comparing the same calendar window (1 Jan–22 May), 2016 sales are about 18% higher than 2015. The business is concentrated: California is ~45% of sales, one store (CA_3) is the clear flagship, and FOODS is still the revenue engine at roughly 58% of sales.

The main planning risk is reading partial years as a crash: 2016 only has 143 days in the training window, so annual totals look small even while per-day demand is strong. Use same-window year-over-year and average daily metrics for leadership reviews.

2. Key findings (from dashboards 08–11)

Network & growth (Executive)

  • Total validated volume: 66.9M units and $191.58M sales across 1,941 sales days.
  • Full calendar years 2012–2015 show sales CAGR about 9.1% (2013 +10%, 2014 +5.4%, 2015 +12%).
  • Category mix: FOODS $111.1M (58%), HOUSEHOLD $57.1M (30%), HOBBIES $23.3M (12%).
  • FOODS share eased slightly over time (~60% early years → ~55% in 2015) as HOUSEHOLD/HOBBIES grew, but FOODS remains primary.

Stores & geography (Store)

  • State mix: CA 44.9% ($85.97M) · TX 28.8% · WI 26.4%.
  • CA_3 leads at $32.70M (17.1% of network); CA_4 is last at $12.47M — about a 2.6× gap.
  • Every store is FOODS-led (~58% average). Extremes: WI_2 highest FOODS (64.5%); CA_2 lowest FOODS / highest HOUSEHOLD (FOODS 49.3%, HOUSEHOLD 38.4%).
  • Average sell price is tight across stores (~$4.34–$4.45), so CA_3’s lead is volume, not a much higher price point.

Products & assortment (Product)

  • All Top 15 SKUs by units sit in department FOODS_3.
  • Head SKU FOODS_3_090: ~1.02M units (~1.5% of all units) and ~$1.38M sales.
  • FOODS is both largest and most volatile on average; HOBBIES is smallest and calmest.
  • FOODS_3 drives sales at a low average price (high-velocity grocery). HOUSEHOLD_2 has extreme max prices (up to $107.32) but far less total sales than FOODS_3.

Demand & calendar (Demand)

  • Rolling demand is upward: mean 7-day average ≈ 34.4K units/day; mean 28-day total ≈ 957K.
  • About 92% of units land on non-event days; labeled event days are only ~8% of units.
  • On a units-per-day basis, M5 “event” days are slightly weaker than non-event days (FOODS −3.3%, HOUSEHOLD −6.9%, HOBBIES −8.7%) — holidays/closures pull averages down.
  • FOODS still dominates volume on both event and non-event days.

4. Decisions and actions to take

  1. Store ops: Treat CA_3 as the benchmark for staffing, replenishment, and assortment. Open a focused review on CA_4 (lowest sales) and CA_2 (highest HOUSEHOLD mix).
  2. Category investment: Put the majority of demand-planning and inventory effort on FOODS, especially department FOODS_3 head SKUs (FOODS_3_090, FOODS_3_586, and the rest of the Top 15).
  3. Leadership metrics: Report growth with average daily units/sales and same-calendar-window YoY. Keep the all-years YoY card (~27%) as context only — it mixes uneven year edges.
  4. Promo & calendar: Plan from non-event baselines. Interpret labeled events with units/day, not stacked totals. Always exclude or specially model 25 Dec in forecasting baselines.
  5. Pricing watch: Use the department price table for assortment reviews — watch HOUSEHOLD_2 high-price outliers while remembering they are not the volume engine.
  6. Operating cadence: After each Gold refresh, re-open the four Power BI pages and this brief; the detailed validation pages (08–11) remain the audit trail if numbers move.
